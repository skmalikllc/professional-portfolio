## Overview

Database and CRM systems that hold a small business's operational records, and the workflows that move data between them.

## Problem

A business grows past the point where a spreadsheet works, buys a CRM or builds an Airtable base, and then discovers the hard part is not the tool. It is that the same customer exists three times under three spellings, the form feeding the base collects the wrong fields, and the automation that was supposed to route new leads stopped firing on a date nobody can identify.

## My role

Build and repair, depending on the engagement — some systems built from scratch, others inherited broken.

## Work performed

- Built Airtable bases with duplicate prevention designed in rather than cleaned up afterwards
- Built and repaired lead-capture and follow-up workflows in a hosted marketing CRM
- Connected forms, CRM, spreadsheets and email through n8n, Make.com and Zapier
- Diagnosed a Make.com and Airtable integration failure end to end
- Applied the patterns that keep these builds alive: validation at the boundary, retries, idempotent reruns, logging, a human approval step where the action is irreversible

## Tools

Airtable · GoHighLevel · n8n · Make.com · Zapier · Jotform · Google Sheets · REST APIs and webhooks

## Solution

Source of truth defined in writing before the first run. Field mapping agreed rather than assumed — most integration failures are a mapping assumption, not a bug. Merge rules for duplicates decided up front. Testing on controlled data, never on live records.

## Outcome

Three completed Airtable engagements are recorded with the client's permission boundaries respected. The CRM service line shows **19 completed engagements, 8 of them rated, every rating observed being 5 stars** — counts verified from the platform record.

Build detail for those CRM engagements is deliberately **not** described: the platform record carries the service line, date, value and rating, not the pipeline configuration. Rather than reconstruct plausible-sounding pipelines from a service title, that repository counts the work and stops there.
