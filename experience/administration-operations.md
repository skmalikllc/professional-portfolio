# Administration & operations experience

Administrative and operational work across both careers — the structured technical environment from 2003 to 2023, and client-facing business operations since 2015.

---

## In the technical operations career (2003 – 2023)

- **Records and documentation.** Maintaining technical and administrative records to a standard where someone who was not present can reconstruct what happened.
- **Operations system records.** Daily work in an internal intranet-based operations and maintenance management system — an ERP-style internal system rather than a commercial ERP product: data entry, inventory records, work orders, maintenance records, material issue and receipt documentation, record tracking and operational reporting, with supervisory oversight of other people's entries.
- **Process compliance.** Working to written SOPs, and contributing to the procedures others then worked from.
- **Scheduling and coordination.** Allocating work across a shift, maintaining continuity at handover.
- **Reporting.** Routine operational reporting to a fixed format and schedule.
- **Correspondence and administration.** General administrative support within a technical function.

## In client-facing work (2015 – present)

- **Client intake.** Designing the forms and the first-contact process, so information arrives complete rather than in fragments across five emails.
- **File architecture.** Folder structures that are still navigable six months later, with naming conventions a team can actually follow.
- **Mailbox and inbox systems.** Labels, filters and rules that keep an overloaded business mailbox usable.
- **Records management.** Contact, lead and customer records kept consistent across systems, with duplicates handled deliberately.
- **Reporting workflows.** Recurring reports assembled and distributed automatically.
- **Handover documentation.** Written so the client's own staff can run the system afterwards.

## The thread between the two

In the technical career the risk was an undocumented change to a maintained system. In client work the risk is an automation that quietly corrupts a record set at 3am. The discipline is the same one: write down what was agreed, work to it, verify the outcome, and hand over something another person can run.
