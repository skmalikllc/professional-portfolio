# Administration & operations experience

Administrative and operational work across both careers — the structured technical environment of the Pakistan Air Force (2003 to 2025), and client-facing business operations since March 2015.

---

## In the Pakistan Air Force career (2003 – 2025)

- **Records and documentation.** Maintaining technical and administrative records, confidential records and filing systems to a standard where someone who was not present can reconstruct what happened; preparing documents for inspections and audits.
- **Operations system records.** Daily work in an internal intranet-based, ERP-style system rather than a commercial ERP product: data management, record verification, inventory records, material issue and receipt documentation and operational reporting, including administering user accounts and access permissions within authorised internal systems.
- **Process compliance.** Working to written SOPs, and contributing to the procedures others then worked from.
- **Training administration.** Trainee registration, enrolment, attendance, training records, completion certificates and examination management (September 2019 – December 2025).
- **Staff coordination and scheduling.** Instructor assignments, duty rosters and course schedules; allocating work and maintaining continuity at handover.
- **Reporting.** Routine operational reporting to a fixed format and schedule.
- **Correspondence and administration.** Official correspondence, notices, administrative reports, meeting minutes, meetings and training events.
- **Additional responsibilities.** Security supervision and access control, mess and catering administration, visitor reception and customer-facing coordination, performed as additional duties rather than separate positions.

## In client-facing work (March 2015 – present)

- **Client intake.** Designing the forms and the first-contact process, so information arrives complete rather than in fragments across five emails.
- **File architecture.** Folder structures that are still navigable six months later, with naming conventions a team can actually follow.
- **Mailbox and inbox systems.** Labels, filters and rules that keep an overloaded business mailbox usable.
- **Records management.** Contact, lead and customer records kept consistent across systems, with duplicates handled deliberately.
- **Reporting workflows.** Recurring reports assembled and distributed automatically.
- **Handover documentation.** Written so the client's own staff can run the system afterwards.

## The thread between the two

In the Pakistan Air Force career the risk was an undocumented change to a maintained system. In client work the risk is an automation that quietly corrupts a record set at 3am. The discipline is the same one: write down what was agreed, work to it, verify the outcome, and hand over something another person can run.
