## Reading the overlap

The two tracks overlap between March 2015 and December 2025, and that is intentional rather than an error.

**2003 – 2025** was the primary career: full-time service in the Pakistan Air Force, beginning with initial technical education and training (2003–2005), then hands-on machinist work and machine shop supervision (April 2005 – September 2019), then technical instruction and training administration (September 2019 – December 2025). Total service: 22 years 4 months.

**March 2015 – December 2025** was part-time freelance work carried alongside it. Remote client services in digital administration, cloud systems, e-commerce support and workflow automation, built up over time outside primary working commitments.

**2025 – present** adds Everlance LLC, which I founded, through which freelance and automation work is also delivered.

**December 2025 – present** is full-time freelance work, following retirement from the Pakistan Air Force. At no point before then was freelancing full-time, and it is not presented as such anywhere in this portfolio.

The 2003–2005 period is initial technical education and training. It is shown under [education](../education/README.md) and is not presented as hands-on machinist employment; the machinist role is dated from April 2005.

---

## Why the transition happened the way it did

A twenty-two-year technical career gives you documentation habits and process discipline. What it does not give you is modern tooling. Building the second track slowly, alongside the first, is how the two ended up reinforcing each other: the automation work is more reliable because of the operational background, and the operational background stayed current because of the automation work.
